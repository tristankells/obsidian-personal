## Chapter 3. What is fat loss?
● Weight loss and fat loss generally go together but are
distinct.
● The bathroom scale is an excellent proxy for progress
but cannot distinguish between changes in fat, muscle,
water or glycogen.
● Body recomposition (building muscle while losing fat)
is possible for those with unrealized muscular
potential, those returning from a layoff or those on
supraphysiological doses of anabolic steroids, making
scale weight an incomplete metric for these groups.
● Muscle preservation should be a central goal of a diet;
any reduction in total body weight should be
strategically engineered to come mainly from fat
stores.

## Chapter 4. The energy balance equation
- Total Daily Energy Expenditure, or TDEE.
	- Four components
		- Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)
			- Resting Energy Expenditure (REE, which includes your BMR plus the energy needs of wakefulness) accounts for roughly sixty to seventy percent of total daily energy expenditure.
		- Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT)
			- This term encompasses all of the calories that you burn through movement that is neither basic biological maintenance—like sleeping and eating—nor intentional, sports-like exercise.
		- The Thermic Effect of Food (TEF)
			- Roughly ten percent of your daily energy intake is lost to the process of digestion, though this varies by macronutrient.
		- Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (EAT)
			- This constitutes the calories burned during intentional physical activity, e.g., lifting weights, running, cycling or taking a yoga class.
			- Surprisingly, for most people, this accounts for only five to ten percent of their TDEE.
- While Calories In, Calories Out (CICO) is the
mechanism of weight loss, the Calories In side is
influenced by factors such as hormones, sleep, stress
and food quality.
- Maintenance calories keep your weight stable, a
calorie surplus makes you gain weight, and a calorie
deficit makes you lose weight.
## Chapter 5. Metabolism myth-busting
- Studies controlling for calories and protein have repeatedly
shown that high-carbohydrate diets (which stimulate more
insulin) result in the same fat loss as low-carbohydrate diets
(which keep insulin low), provided that energy intake is equal.
Insulin partitions energy, but it cannot create energy
out of thin air.
- Sleep deprivation, for example, has
been shown to lower leptin and raise ghrelin, increasing
hunger and cravings.
- Starvation mode is a myth; while metabolic adaptation
causes energy expenditure to drop as you lose weight,
this does not halt fat loss in a calorie deficit.
- Your genetics do set your susceptibility to the modern
obesogenic environment (hyper-palatable food, low
activity), but conscious, strategic effort can override
genetic predispositions to eat.
- Hormones (such as insulin, thyroid, leptin and ghrelin)
influence metabolism and appetite, but they operate
within the confines of the energy balance equation;
they can be managed through medical treatment when
impaired, alongside lifestyle factors such as a high
protein intake, resistance training, volume eating and
adequate sleep.

## ## Chapter 8. Determining maintenance calories
- Do you want to lose 0.5 kg (1.1 lb) of fat per week? You
need a daily deficit of ~550 calories.
- Do you want to lose 1.0 kg (2.2 lb) of fat per week? You
need a daily deficit of ~1,100 calories.
- When the diet ends, it helps
to have a precise calorie target to return to so that you can
stop losing weight without immediately rebounding into a
surplus.
8.5. Key takeaways
● Determining your true maintenance calories (TDEE) is
valuable for control and predictability. It allows you to
set a precise daily deficit for a target rate of fat loss and
gives you a landing strip to prevent a post-diet
rebound.
● Generic calculators are a good starting point but can
be inaccurate; TDEE is better established by tracking
your data either through a two-week maintenance test
or by a test-as-you-go method.

## Chapter 10. Protein requirements for fat loss
- dynamic equilibrium between two opposing forces:
Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS), the building of new tissue,
and Muscle Protein Breakdown (MPB), the degradation of
old tissue.
- amino acid leucine (from protein) initiates the signalling pathway that
stimulates MPS to counteract the deficit-driven drop,
- Standard nutritional guidelines for protein have typically
hovered around the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)
of 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day.
- researchers demonstrated
that during prolonged starvation, lean people experience
protein loss and oxidation rates that are two to three times
higher than those seen in obese people,
- your body fat decreases, your dietary protein intake should
increase to offset the body’s growing tendency to burn
muscle for fuel.
- protein needs under calorie restriction are
substantially higher than at maintenance, estimating an
optimal range of **2.3 to 3.1 grams per kilogram of Fat-Free**
**Mass (FFM).**
- Hector and Phillips... establishing a general optimal
intake range of **1.6 to 2.4 grams per kilogram** of total body
weight per day,
- the benefits of pushing protein beyond the
standard optimum include: (1) a higher thermic effect of food
to increase energy expenditure subtly; (2) enhanced satiety;
(3) a protective effect on the athlete’s mood; and (4) an extra
layer of insurance for muscle retention.
- Further, there is a literal financial cost to consider. Protein is
universally the most expensive macronutrient per gram.
- if you wish to free up more of your calorie budget
for carbohydrates and fats, then you can safely scale your
protein back to 1.4–2.0 g/kg of total body weight upon
transitioning out of your fat-loss phase.

## Determining Maintenance Calories
### Method A: the two-week maintenance test
- Use the **Mifflin-St Jeor** equation as rough estimate.
	- Quick and dirty: Multiply your **weight in kilograms by 30 to 33.**
- For 14 days, consume this exact amount. 
	- Strictly weigh and tracking every gram of food ((chapter 21 details how to do)
	- Weigh yourself every morning, after using the toilet but before consuming any food or water.
- On day 15, you will calculate the average weight of week 2 and compare it against the average of week 1 (chapter 27 details how to do this).
	- If stable, no more than 0.1 to 0.2 kg, then that is maintenance.
	- Average trended up, then you are likely in a surplus.
	- If it trended down, then you are likely in a deficit.
	- Assume that every 0.1 kg of weekly weight change equals an approximate 110-calorie daily discrepancy.
	- So, if you gained 0.4 kg on average each week, then you can assume that you were in a daily ~440-calorie surplus, meaning that your maintenance is your intake minus that amount.
### Method B: the test-as-you-go method
- To begin, you need a starting calorie target that puts you in a likely deficit; 
	- A Protein-Sparing Modified Fast (PSMF; see chapter 14) would fall into this category.
	- Quick and dirty: Multiply your **weight in kilograms by 26.**
- Strictly track food and weigh self.
- Week 1 will not be representitive because of water weight, but from then onwards you can apply the following formula to get TDEE.
	- TDEE = average daily intake + ((reference weight − current weight) / number of weeks ) × 1100
Example:
● Week 2 average (reference): 90.0 kg.
● Week 4 average (current): 89.0 kg.
● Timeframe: 2 weeks.
● Calculation: (90.0 - 89.0) / 2 = 0.5 kg lost per week.
● Daily deficit: 0.5 kg × 1,100 = 550 calories.
● Average daily intake: 2,340 calories.
● TDEE (maintenance): 2,340 (intake) + 550 (deficit) =
2,890 calories.
- As you get leaner, your maintenance will likely drop. you can review
any recent 14- or 21-day block and recalculate your
maintenance.
- You should consider opting for the two-week maintenance
test if you are a novice to tracking macronutrients. If you
have never weighed food before, then attempting to learn
these skills (see chapter 21) while enduring the hunger of a
deficit can be burdensome and demoralizing; the two-week
maintenance test allows you to develop accuracy in a
relatively low-stress environment. It is also the prescription
for those with a history of yo-yo dieting. If you have spent
years cycling between starvation and bingeing, then you may
benefit from a period of time to repair your relationship with
food before the stress of a deficit is introduced (indeed, in
these circumstances, the two-week timeline can and should
be extended for as long as it takes until you feel stable and in
control of your eating habits). Further, if you are prone to
anxiety regarding whether you are cutting too hard or not enough, then the two-week maintenance test will help by
removing all doubt before you begin.
### Your personal maintenance multiplier
- To determine, divide TDEE by current weight:
Example:
● Current weight: 90.0 kg
● Calculated maintenance: 2,890 calories
● The calculation: 2,890 / 90 = 32.1 kcal/kg
next time you decide to diet—whether simply take your new body weight and multiply it by "Your personal maintenance multiplier" find out your updated maintenance calories instantly.
- less accurate if your lifestyle changes drastically (e.g., transitioning from an active job to a desk job) or if you substantially alter your ratio of muscle to body fat,
	- better and more individualized starting point than any generic formula you will find on the internet.

## US Navy method for estimating fat percentage
How to measure:
- **Neck:** measure the circumference of the neck. For men, measure just below the larynx (Adam’s apple). For women, measure at the mid-neck. Keep the tape flat and tight enough to touch the skin but not compress it.
- **Abdomen/waist:**
	- *Men*: measure the circumference of the abdomen at the level of the navel (belly button).
	- *Women*: measure the waist circumference at the narrowest point, usually halfway between the navel and the bottom of the sternum.
- **Hips (women only):** measure the circumference of the hips at the widest part of the buttocks.
- **Height:** measure your height without shoes.
- Once you have these raw numbers, do not attempt to do the maths yourself; the formula involves logarithmic calculations that are tedious to perform by hand. Simply search for a ‘US Navy body fat calculator’ online, plug in your measurements and use the result to determine your body fat percentage.
